| Claim in the film | Source |

| The kingfisher's long, narrow beak enters water with little splash | AskNature, "Beak provides streamlining" https://asknature.org/strategy/beak-provides-streamlining/ ; Bangor University https://bangor.ac.uk/news/latest/researching-the-kingfisher-s-iconic-hydrodynamic-design-40708 |
| 500 Series: a 15 m nose, almost round in cross-section; 30 % less air pressure, 15 % less electricity, 10 % faster than the former series | Japan for Sustainability interview with Eiji Nakatsu, 31 March 2005 https://www.japanfs.org/en/news/archives/news_id027795.html |
| 500 Series entered service in 1997 | JR Pass (above) |
| Densities: air ≈ 1.2 kg/m³, water ≈ 1,000 kg/m³ (approximate, at room conditions) | standard physical values |

Caveat: the kingfisher link is a widely told design account (from Nakatsu himself), not a documented engineering study. The film presents it as his inspiration, not as a measured experiment.
