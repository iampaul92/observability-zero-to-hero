# The Tea Merchant's Road — treatment

Style: **Ukiyo-e** (woodblock prints brought to life). Vertical 1080×1920, 24 fps, ~60 s, English narration with cartouche subtitles.
Tall prints are native to the medium (hashira-e, "pillar prints"), so the vertical frame is one tall sheet.

## 1. Three candidate structures

1. **The road as a handscroll.** Prints mounted side by side; the camera slides right → left from station to station. Each slide is shorter than the last, so the journey speeds up toward the city, then stops dead. *Clear, travels well vertically, and the slides themselves become the rhythm.*
2. **Alternate impressions of one doorway.** Only the home doorway is shown, reprinted for each season while the narrator tells what happened on the road off-screen. *Elegant, but the strangers he meets are never seen, so the kindness has no faces.*
3. **One bowl, a series of views.** Every print is a close view of the bowl in a different place (a ferry plank, snow, a lantern stall, a lacquer tray). *A strong constant subject, but the bowl is small and the faces matter more.*

**Chosen: 1, turned vertical** (see §9), **with the bowl as the constant subject inside each print (from 3) and the doorway's alternate impression as the ending (from 2).** The handscroll gives the journey and its acceleration; the bowl in each print ties the stations together; the alternate-impression doorway makes the ending echo the opening.

## 2. Logline and arc

A young merchant carries his grandmother's plain, crooked tea bowl to the capital to sell it, but each stranger he pours tea for on the way makes it worth more than gold.

- **Setup:** dawn, the family doorway. Kenji leaves with the bowl in a cloth bundle.
- **Journey:** rain at the river ferry, snow on the mountain pass, a festival night. Each time someone is cold or lost, he pours them tea from the bowl.
- **Turn:** in the city, a rich man offers ten gold coins. Silence. The print washes back to blank paper; only the bowl's key line remains, and the three colours of the road print onto it one by one.
- **Ending:** the same doorway, reprinted at dusk in autumn with a lantern lit. Kenji pours the first cup for his grandmother. The print fades to paper; only the red seal stays.

## 3. Benchmark

- **Hiroshige's *Fifty-three Stations of the Tōkaidō*** as a series: learn how a journey is told as stations, weather carrying the mood, tiny figures against big weather. Don't take any composition.
- ***The Tale of the Princess Kaguya* (2013)**: learn restraint: unprinted paper as emotion, a held silence before the turn. Don't take characters, designs or shots.

## 6. Cue map

- Free-rhythm shakuhachi over a slow shamisen ostinato at **72 bpm**, miyako-bushi scale on D (D E♭ G A B♭).
- 0–10.5: shakuhachi alone over a wind bed; a single shamisen sawari note when the seal stamps (0.3 s).
- 10.5–33: shamisen ostinato enters; shime-daiko taps added at the festival; each slide lands on a clapper (hyōshigi) hit.
- 33–38.4: taiko heartbeat tightening under the offer.
- 38.4–40.2: **absolute silence** (reverb tails cut). The first sound after it is one koto note as the first colour block lands.
- 40.2–46: three koto notes, one per block (blue, white, red), then a koto glissando into the dusk doorway.
- 46–57: shakuhachi returns with the opening phrase, resolved; wind chime.
- 57–61: hyōshigi accelerating to the curtain call, then one bell as the seal remains.

- Subtitles: a cream horizontal cartouche with a thick + thin sumi border and a small red seal at the left, Shippori Mincho 500, near the bottom; prints in with a baren-sweep mask, fades out. Figures and ground lines stay above it.

## 9. Changes after comparing with the style's demo (DIRECTOR.md §11)

The demo is also a journey told as a handscroll whose slides speed up, so two of the six points matched. Changed:

- **Camera path.** The prints hang one below another on a tall hanging scroll (kakemono), and the camera travels *down* it (1.6 s → 1.2 s → 0.9 s), with a hard cut to the city instead of a fourth slide. Pushes toward each pour; a crash-zoom into the bowl's glaze carries us into the last print.
- **Score shape.** No build to a drum roll: the music is fullest at the festival, then *stops* on the cut to the city, leaving one low shamisen note and a soft heartbeat, then absolute silence. The turn is three single koto notes.

Opening (close crop on the bowl), signature shot (the road's colours printing onto the bowl), ending (the alternate impression of the doorway, then the seal alone) and the structure's constant subject already differed. Five of six now differ.
